**What "passes locally, fails on CI" tells us**

The test logic itself probably works. Something differs between your machine and the CI runner. Ranked by how often this turns out to be the cause:

1. **Different code.** Your local checkout may not have Marcin's change; CI tests the merged branch. Your local "pass" is then just old code passing. Check: `git fetch` then `git status -sb` in the repo. If it says you're behind, this is likely it.
2. **Env vars.** Local `.env` sets something CI doesn't. Stripe-specific: a pinned `STRIPE_API_VERSION` locally vs. account default in CI changes the webhook payload shape, and a strict schema rejects the new shape with 422.
3. **Timezone.** CI runs in UTC, your machine doesn't. If the test builds a payload containing dates, validation can pass at your local time and fail at UTC.
4. **Test order or parallelism.** CI runs tests in a different order or in parallel; another test mutates shared state before this one runs.
5. **Dependencies.** CI cache or an unpinned lockfile installs a different library version that serializes the payload differently.

One more thing that makes this easier than it sounds: a FastAPI 422 response includes a JSON body naming the exact field that failed validation. The CI log almost certainly printed it right next to the `expected 200 got 422` line.
